# P.O.L.A.R. Production Voice & Media Standard v1

## Purpose

Prevent regression into generic robotic, trailer-narrator, or synthetic corporate delivery and define how P.O.L.A.R. dialogue is timed against animation.

## Character voice

P.O.L.A.R. character dialogue uses a young male voice with a slightly higher register, friendly and inviting delivery, natural contractions, quick intelligence, and conversational pacing.

Required qualities:
- youthful male timbre
- slightly higher register than a conventional corporate narrator
- warm, friendly, intelligent delivery
- natural phrasing and contractions
- subtle personality without cartoon exaggeration
- responsive conversational cadence
- relaxed, human pauses
- clear en-US pronunciation

Reject:
- deep movie-trailer narration
- generic corporate spokesman delivery
- monotone or flat cadence
- exaggerated announcer pauses
- synthetic assistant inflection
- over-enunciated punctuation
- improv-robot or call-center cadence

## Timing rule

Voice drives scene duration. Spoken dialogue is generated or selected first, then animation is authored to fit the real audio duration with adequate lead-in and tail-out.

Do not force multi-second dialogue into micro-transition footage.

### Transition clips

Short navigation/department transitions are visual/SFX stings. They should normally remain around 0.75–1.25 seconds and contain no sentence-length dialogue. Optional speech is limited to a single short label or interjection that comfortably fits the source duration.

### Dialogue clips

Greeting, explainer, onboarding, idle inquiry, guidance, and response clips must use intentionally long enough animation windows. Their visual duration should be matched to approved voice audio rather than time-stretched to arbitrary legacy footage.

## Production sequence

SCRIPT → VOICE BENCHMARK → AUDIO APPROVAL → TIMING MAP → APPROVED ASSET RETRIEVAL → ANIMATION → LIP/MOTION SYNC → MIX → QC → EXPORT → RELEASE

## Benchmarking

A production voice is not considered approved because a render completed. Approval requires comparison against the current P.O.L.A.R. reference direction and a human listening check. Failed, partial, duration-mismatched, or corrupted renders are never promoted as voice references.

## Asset rules

Use only currently approved BPEI corporate masters and P.O.L.A.R. character masters. Rejected or superseded character imagery must not be reintroduced by generation, fallback selection, or template reuse.

## Voice governance

The voice profile is a controlled brand asset. Provider IDs may be stored only in approved server-side configuration or governed asset metadata. Raw credentials and reusable authentication secrets never enter prompts, public repositories, or ordinary institutional memory.

## Distribution

Publishing a rendered asset remains approval-gated. Drafting, timing analysis, quality checks, and preparation may be autonomous within authorized scope.
